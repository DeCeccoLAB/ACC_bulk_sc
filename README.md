## Beyond Receptor Tyrosine Kinases: Redefining Therapeutic Vulnerabilities in Adenoid Cystic Carcinoma

**Background**: Adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) comprises two molecular subtypes: ACC-I, with poor prognosis, and the more indolent ACC-II. Emerging therapies, including EGFR/MET-directed antibodies, MYB-targeting agents, and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), raise the question of how drug-relevant targets are distributed across these subtypes.  

**Methods**: Eight public bulk transcriptomic datasets (266 ACC, 26 normal salivary tissues) were harmonized, and tumors were assigned to ACC-I/ACC-II. Twelve clinically targeted genes were compared across tissues and subtypes by Wilcoxon tests and ROC analysis. Associations with event-free survival (EFS) were assessed by Cox regression, with and without subtype adjustment. Single-cell data (10 samples; 1,725 cells) defined cellular localization and transcriptional programs of target-negative cells.  

**Results**: ACC-I and ACC-II accounted for 35.3% and 64.7% of tumors. VTCN1, CDK6, MYB, and KIT were strongly overexpressed in both subtypes (AUC versus normal, 0.94–0.98), whereas EGFR (AUC 0.92), TACSTD2, and MET best discriminated ACC-II from ACC-I. High TACSTD2, EGFR, and MET expression was associated with longer EFS (HR 0.41–0.45), but these associations were largely attenuated after subtype adjustment; only MET retained an association within ACC-II, and KIT was the only subtype-independent prognostic gene. Most targets localized to cancer cells, whereas FAP and STING1 were predominantly stromal; target-negative cells showed distinct programs, mainly epithelial–mesenchymal transition and interferon responses.  

**Conclusions**: Transcriptomics adds drug-relevant resolution within the ACC-I/ACC-II framework. Because receptor- and ADC-antigen-rich tumors are enriched in prognostically favorable ACC-II, early-phase trials should report subtype distribution and measure drug-relevant features directly to separate treatment effect from case mix.
